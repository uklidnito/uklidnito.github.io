5 Quick Exercises

Light stretches and movement you can do anywhere in a few minutes, no equipment needed — at work, at home, or on the go. Pairs perfectly with the Uklidni To breathing exercise for an even faster reset.

Why a short stretch helps

Stress and anxiety don't just live in your head — they settle into your muscles too, most often in the neck, shoulders and back. A few minutes of light movement helps the body release built-up tension and signals to the nervous system that the alarm can switch off. The exercises below are deliberately short and simple, so you can fit them in anytime — on their own, or as a warm-up before a breathing exercise.

5 exercises for fast calming

1Neck stretch

30–45 seconds

Sit or stand up straight, shoulders relaxed away from your ears. Slowly tilt your head toward your right shoulder until you feel a gentle stretch on the left side of your neck. Hold for 15–20 seconds, then return slowly and switch sides.

Releases tension in the neck — one of the first places stress settles.

2Shoulder rolls

20–30 seconds

Let your arms hang loosely at your sides. Slowly roll your shoulders backward in big, slow circles — up, back, down and forward. After 5–8 rounds, reverse the direction.

Gets blood flowing through the upper back and helps drop tension from hunched shoulders.

3Standing forward fold

30–60 seconds

Stand with feet hip-width apart. Slowly bend forward from the hips, knees softly bent, letting your head and arms hang loosely toward the floor. Let your head and neck fully relax, breathing calmly.

Stretches the back and neck — the inverted head position briefly calms the nervous system.

4Shaking out hands & legs

20–30 seconds

Stand up and gently shake out your hands from the wrists, as if flicking off water, then add your whole arms. Next, balance on one leg and shake out the other, then switch sides.

A fast physical reset that helps discharge built-up nervousness and adrenaline.

5Knees to chest

30–60 seconds

Lie on your back (or sit), pull both knees toward your chest and gently hug them with your arms. Let your head rest, relaxed, and breathe slowly into your belly.

Gently stretches the lower back and creates a sense of safety, similar to a curled-up resting position.

💡 Tip: These work best done back-to-back as a short 2–3 minute routine, followed by a few minutes of the guided Uklidni To breathing exercise — first release the body, then calm the breath and mind.

Frequently asked questions

How long should calming exercises take?

Just 2–3 minutes is enough. Pick a single exercise for the area that feels most tense, or run through all five as a short routine.

Do I need to do the exercises in a specific order?

No. The order doesn't matter — pick whichever one you feel like, or whichever part of your body is holding the most tension right now.

Are these exercises suitable for the office or school?

Yes. All of them can be done sitting or standing in regular clothes, with no equipment, right at your desk or during a break.

Can I combine this with the Uklidni To breathing exercise?

Yes, it's an ideal combination. Start with a few minutes of stretching to release the body, then follow with the guided breathing exercise to calm the mind.
